The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚔ
A TypeScript playfulness experiment — a small game-flavored piece built in TypeScript as an exercise in charm: power-ups, pickups, the small delight-mechanics that make a toy worth fiddling with. It is TypeScript end to end, the types doing real work against game-shaped state, and it is deliberately slight: a plaything, not a product, built to be finished rather than maintained. It exists to prove a small point to the gardener himself — that a charm of an idea can be caught quickly, held completely, and let go again without ceremony.

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

Aengus of the Birds dreamt of a swan-girl nine nights running and then searched Ireland a full year for the shape of the dream — up the Boyne, into the sídhe, until at Caer Ibormeith the dream stood on the water wearing chains of gold, and even then the winning of her took another negotiation of forms. The garden's own dream was a smaller charm and asked less of the world: a power-up, a bright coin of a thing, dreamed up and chased through TypeScript's typed fields in a season of evenings. It was not a year of searching — the charm was cornered in a handful of commits, each one typed and each one true. But the shape of the hunt is the same in both tellings: something shines, something is desired, the pursuer goes into the strange field after it and does not come out unchanged. The stone is named for the modern word for the old shining, and the moss grows on it brightest at dusk.
