The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚐ
Custom model definitions for Open WebUI — a shelf of named vessels for shapes of thought, each one a way of ladling the same fire into a different cup. Each definition is written in Python for Ollama, the household's own model pot, and served through Open WebUI: a keener for some questions, a jester for others, a clerk for the rest — the same pot, called by different names. It exists because a cauldron's whole virtue is that no guest leaves unsatisfied, and one ladle — one model, one manner — was never going to manage that alone.

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

The Tuatha Dé Danann were never one shape — the same spirit went about as a hag at the ford, a warrior on the road, a salmon under the weir, and the old texts are careful to say these were not deceits but true forms, the way a fire is truly a hearth and truly a bonfire at once. The model definitions on this shelf were cut in that tradition: one house of inference, many named vessels, each definition ladling the same fire into a different cup — a keener for the grieving questions, a jester for the idle ones, a clerk for everything that must be filed. The pot itself is the household's own — Ollama's, served through Open WebUI — and the definitions hang from the beam like recipes, in Python, each one answering when called by name. The Dagda's cauldron was praised for a single property: that no guest ever left it unsatisfied, and that it never emptied, not even after the cow-raid. A shelf of named forms is how a modern cauldron keeps that promise. The ladle comes out slightly different each time the moss is brushed aside, which the guests have learned to call flavor.
