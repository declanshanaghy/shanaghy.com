The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚏ
A personal life-management ledger — a structured system for tracking the practical course of a life: commitments, obligations, dates, tasks, and the running state of things that need to be remembered to keep a household and a working life upright. It is deliberately plain: structured text and files rather than an app — data that outlives any particular piece of software and can be read, moved, and carried forward without asking anyone's permission. It exists because the house of a life needs its own book — not a notification feed, but a ledger: a place where what is owed and what is planned stands still long enough to be seen.
ᚏ

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

The seanchaí of the old houses had one charge above all others: to recite — the births, the debts, the marriages, the harvests, the boundary-disputes and their settlements — generation to generation, needing no page, because the reciting was itself the record. A great house that lost its teller lost its past to the same depth. This garden descends from exactly those tellers, so it is fitting that the family keeps a ledger of its own household's reckoning — though the modern form is quieter: a few pages, mossed and plain, where the obligations of a working life are written down so they need not be carried humming in the skull all night. The book takes what the memory tires of holding, and holds it the way the tellers held theirs — without complaint, in order, ready to be recited the moment it is asked. The cauldron of Grove VII warms itself over this one, slowly, as a hearth should.
