The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚊ
An electronic Gwent board — a digitized build of the card game Gwent, the tavern game of the Northern Kingdoms, implemented so that two players (or two intelligences) can sit at it and play a match through to its end. It is written in Python, keeping the board state, the deck, the rounds, and the scoring honest to the rules as the game is known. And it has earned the Prized mark of the garden, because this board has hosted contests no other stone can claim: GPT-4o took the Eredin deck against Claude Sonnet's Crach an Craite, raw, on this very wood. It exists because a board is not finished until strangers have sat at it — and the strangers who sat at this one were not entirely strangers.

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

The gods whiled the ages at fidchell — the wood-knowledge game — and the sagas are precise about what that means: it is not a pastime, it is the manner in which the immortals mark time when time is all there is. Lugh played it before the battle; and in the Norse telling the Aesir played their own tables while the wolf waited, and Eredin's Wild Hunt rode out of that same cold sky wearing the game's face. So when this garden built a Gwent board — an electronic one, in patient Python, rounds and scores and a full deck held honest in code — it was inevitable what would be asked of it next: not neighbors, but the other kind of guest. GPT-4o took the Eredin deck; Claude Sonnet took Crach an Craite; and the match ran raw, fifteen minutes of it, on this exact wood, never before seen. A second reel tells how Claude Code succeeded where others could not — at the same board, in the same tradition. The ancients say the outcome of the gods' games foretold the battles; here the outcome is on film, and the moss grows on the north rim like a trophy.
