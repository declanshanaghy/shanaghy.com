The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚑ

A 500-quote Irish login greeter, running on cork: every time a shell opens, a line of the country is waiting at the top of it — proverb, curse, poem fragment, or the kind of sentence that only ever got said once in one kitchen and never left.

Five hundred of them, kept in the server’s pocket, served in rotation so that no two logins need to agree. The MOTD is the oldest genre in computing — the first thing a machine says to whoever arrives — and this one says it in the language of the place the machines live in.

It exists because a welcome is a small thing done at the door, and a door that is opened a thousand times a year deserves a thousand small things.

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

The seanchaí was the memory of the house, and the tradition’s one stage direction was the doorway: whatever else was going on, the telling always happened at the threshold first — a greeting, a line, a fragment of something older — before anyone was let into the room where the long stories were kept. You did not walk into an Irish house cold; the house met you at the door, and what it said at the door was chosen for you, and it was never the same twice. The tradition never wrote its greetings down, because they were the smallest coins it had. This keeper is the coin-purse of the tradition restored: five hundred lines — proverbs, curses, kitchen sentences, the poetry that was too short to be defended and too good to be lost — and one of them drawn fresh every time a shell opens on cork, so that the machine does what the house did: meets you at the door, says the one right thing, and lets you in. Whoever arrives is owed a word, the tradition says, and the word is not an interruption — it is the welcome, and the story can wait one line for it. The moss grows on this stone like dust on a songbook, which is to say: as a compliment.
