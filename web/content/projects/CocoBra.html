The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚅ

An Arduino experiment, woven — wire and thread instead of PCB and breadboard. The circuit is not housed but plaited: conductive paths laid in the weave, the components worked into the braid like beads into hair.

Weaving is an old word for what circuits do anyway — a track crossed at intervals by another, held under tension, the whole pattern decided before the first thread. The Arduino in this one animates the braid: light and sensing carried by the cloth itself.

It exists because e-textiles are the oldest electronics that were ever wearable, and because the bench wanted to know what happened when the circuit and the cloth were the same object.

ᚏ

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

At Bealtaine and at Imbolc, in the old houses, the women plaited — rushes into crosses, thread into crios belts, straw into harmony knots — and the pattern was never decoration, it was ward: the plait as protection, the interlacing as a way of holding a thing closed against whatever the season sent. A crossing is a promise. When the bench in 2013 decided to plait a circuit, it was doing this old work in a new material: the wire is a rush that happens to conduct, the weave is a cross that happens to compute, and the Arduino animates the ward — the plait not just holding the dark out but answering to it, which no rush cross ever managed. The old craft would have called that an upgrade, not a sacrilege: the purpose of a weave is to keep, and a weave that keeps and speaks keeps better. Cross the threads, says every plait ever made in a season of need. Tension, tension, hold — and whatever comes, the pattern is what survives you. The moss grows on this stone exactly like moss on a well-worked basket: in the grooves, following the plait, in on itself.
