The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚓ
A Raspberry Pi tending the BBQ — the fire-box brought under instrument: a Pi set at the iron ring of the grill, watching the coals and reporting on the meat like a farmer at the gate. It is written in Go, running on the Raspberry Pi, sensors at the pit — the small board doing the fire-tender's office: watching, waiting, and calling out before the meat is wrong. It exists because a fire unwatched is a feast gambled, and the old cooking-places never left the vigil to luck. This one delegates the vigil to a machine that does not blink.

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

Under the furze of every good hunting ground in old Ireland, the diggers keep finding the same thing: a pit lined with timber, filled with water, and beside it the scorched stones that were heated in the fire and rolled in to boil it — the fulacht fia, the cooking-place of the Fianna, where the hunted meat of the day was made the feast of the night. It was the first instrumentation of the household fire: the heat measured in stones, the vigil kept by whoever was not hunting. This garden rebuilt the arrangement with what the age provides — a Raspberry Pi, the Go tongue, sensors set at the iron ring of the grill — so the pit now keeps its own counsel and reports the fire like a farmer at the gate. The Pi does what the fire-tender did at the fulacht: watches, waits, and calls out before the meat is wrong. The Bealtaine priests blessed the herds and the year's first meat at just such fires; this one is smaller, and better watched. The camera has come to this pit twice already, and both reels are planted below; the moss smells faintly of smoke, which is not encouraged, but neither is it corrected.
