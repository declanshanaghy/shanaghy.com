The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚅ

StormBox redux — the storm, brewed again. The second build keeps the first’s creed — lightning, timing, a closed lid — and improves on every promise: longer brewed, cleaner bursts, an apparatus that learned from its own weather.

The tradition of the second attempt is the oldest tradition on any bench: the first build teaches you what you meant, and the second build is the one that gets to mean it. The differences are all in the brewing — the flash held longer, the random less random, the case better sealed.

It exists because no storm is ever told right the first time.

ᚏ

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

The seanchaí will tell you there is a rule about telling, and it is not modesty: no story is told right the first time. The first telling teaches the teller the shape of the thing; the second telling is the one the listeners were owed. The whole Battle of Mag Tuired — the most important battle in the Irish calendar of stories — was, the old books admit, fought twice: once badly, once again, the second round taking a year to prepare and the turf it was fought on remembered ever since as if one battle happened. The garden’s storm follows the rule exactly. The first box, 2011, was the storm as a first telling: eager, ragged, flashes stepping on each other’s heels, a squall with no discipline. The redux, a year later, is the storm told right — the same weather, brewed longer, each burst given its own full silence to burst out of, the case sealed the way a second telling seals its joints. Once for the teller, says the rule. Twice for the tale. The moss grows on the second box the thickest, which is the story’s own commentary on which telling mattered.
