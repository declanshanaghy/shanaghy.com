The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚅ

A storm in a box — an early build. LEDs for lightning, thunder optional, all of it cased: weather as a hobby project, where the garden first learned to put a phenomenon in a housing and close the lid.

The storm is Java’s: timing the flashes, randomizing the intervals, doing what storms do which is not be predictable. The box is the workshop’s: a front panel, a switch, and the whole apparatus tame on a shelf.

It exists because weather is the one thing everyone tries to own eventually, and a box is the smallest legal container for it.

ᚏ

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

Manannán mac Lir, the story goes, could fold the weather — take a squall off the sea and pleat it small enough to carry in a pocket, and shake it out again over whatever fleet deserved it. The gods of the coasts kept weather the way householders keep salt: something you did not use all at once, something you stored. This stone is the Athlone bench’s first attempt at the same housekeeping, and it dates from 2011, when the maker was still at the stage where every project teaches a verb — and this one taught contain. A storm, done small, is mostly a matter of timing: the flash that is too soon to expect, the darkness after it, the order of the two. Java held the timing; the box held the storm; the shelf held the box, which is the whole chain of custody that the gods never wrote down. Weather you can’t predict is just weather, says the folded squall. Weather you can predict, and then don’t tell anyone — that is power. The moss grew on the lid like the felt on a piano no one is playing, which in a storm-box’s case is the correct state.
