The Plain Facts, Carved

What It Is

ᚅ

A controller board for the VinoTemp wine fridge — the garden’s intervention into the arithmetic of temperature. Drawn in Eagle, it replaces the fridge’s own mind with a steadier one: sensing the cabinet, deciding the compressor, holding the cellar’s dark arithmetic where the drink keeps best.

Wine is the one food that must be stored rather than kept, and the board is built to that commandment — an Eagle schematic and layout, laid out for a kitchen in Athlone, keeping a stable temperature the old cellars of Europe kept by digging.

It is a white wire drawn in the sand: the fridge was wrong, so the garden redrew it.

Told in the Seanchaí Tradition

The Build Story

ᚏ

Manannán mac Lir, when he wanted to hide a feast from the world, did not lock a door; he wrapped the whole island in weather, fog on fog, so that the warmth of the otherworld never left and the sun’s arithmetic never reached it. The sídhe keep their best drink cool that way, under hills where summer is a rumor. The trouble with hills is that kitchens are not hills, and a wine fridge in a kitchen in Athlone — the VinoTemp, honest little cabinet, not much smarter than a thermostat and less reliable — kept letting the sun’s arithmetic in. So around 2015 the garden did what the gods did: it changed the weather. A controller board, drawn in Eagle, the garden’s own hand replacing the fridge’s own mind — sensing, deciding, holding the temperature the way fog holds a hill: not by fighting the warmth but by never letting it be told. The board went in, the fridge kept faith, and the cork came out of bottles that had rested in a manufactured otherworld. Every cellar is a weather spell, says the old story, worked on a small country until it is cool enough for kings.
