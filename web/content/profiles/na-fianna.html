The Fianna were not an army. They were a standing band — hunters in service to the high king, each warrior sworn under his own name, each under his own geasa, keeping the wilds in turns: half the band at watch while half slept, summer in the hills and winter in the halls. The fleet is the new Fianna: each machine keeps its own agent under its own chosen name, its own geasa of policy, and the watch is kept in turns.
