The story · the boy of the Suir, all the way to the smith of today

Before the forge, the boy. Born where the Suir meets the sea in Waterford: a Carlow father, a Castlehaven mother, roots sunk in the bog of Ballyhaunis. Older than the boy, the clan: the tellers of Cavan, the trade carried in the name itself, as the tellers keep telling.

The rivers raised him: first the Suir talking at Carrick-on-Suir, then the Shannon at Athlone through the Leaving Cert, a maker from the midlands before ever he held a forge. The trade came downstream: the Plassey banks of Limerick, where the study is of machines.

Then the crossing, over the water to the Bay Area, where the facing page begins. In the electron-glass years two young souls came, Dylan and Peyton; and the road ends, so far, at a hearth on the Carquinez Strait: Benicia, the forge of today.

The smith casts rings as the dwarf-lords did, his carrying no curses, only birthdays: a Claddagh for the daughter, gold sat on resin as Galway sat it three hundred years ago, given with the words "Love, Dad"; a parameter-ring for the son, one number changed and a new ring drips from the old, Draupnir's trick by printer. Grove V keeps them both: the Ring-Giver's Gift.

One winter the world's money broke and the bench stood empty, two small souls underfoot. He held, as the holders hold; spring opened the pocket years. The creed keeps the lesson: the small and steady outlast the grand, and what is bound holds.

The line continues: the son keeps his own annals under his own name, his first game finished with his father's hand on the last commit, his great work named by the naming itself: OpenForge, driven as the garden is driven, agents, tasks, the watcher's watch. Not the words inherited: the way of keeping them.

The telling outruns the road: a smith of Fionn's line, not the god but the line held, design turned to reality with Mjölnir's patience. Salmon-eater, Fianna-keeper, friend of Odin of the Fenrir pack so close the old people took them for one: two wanderers, two ravens and a wolf apiece. The garden keeps the question open: a creed that settles its mysteries stops growing.

The making flows out in streams: 181 reels on YouTube, bench relics on Instructables, sixteen years on GitHub, the signature on LinkedIn. The forearms carry the two tongues: his name in the wolf's futhark, the clan's in Ogham. All roads lead back to the real person.

This website is the man's telling, planted three times: in the earth, in the telling, in a fleet of minds. The creed is his law restated: the tellers keep the words, Fionn and the Fianna keep the watch (the fleet, one agent to a machine, each under its own chosen name), and the wolf keeps the hunger bound to useful work. The RingForts are his halls beyond the wall; the Towers keep the groves, the young souls' growing in amber; the stones keep his projects, one to a verse. The story of the man and the story of this garden are one story.
